title: ProxySQL
description: Put ProxySQL in front of a MariaDB database for connection pooling and read/write routing, connect an app to it, and see its backends and query rules.
---

**ProxySQL** sits between your apps and a **MariaDB** database. Apps connect to ProxySQL, which keeps a pool of connections to MariaDB and routes each query to a backend. Add a [MariaDB](/databases/mariadb) to the environment first.

## Add ProxySQL

<Steps>

<Step>

### Pick it from the menu

On the canvas, click **+ Service**, then **Add-on** (**Kafka, email, and more**), then **ProxySQL** ("Connection pooling and read/write split for MariaDB").

<Screenshot id="proxysql/1-menu" alt="The Add-on menu: Memcached, ProxySQL, Email (SMTP) and Scheduled tasks" />

</Step>

<Step>

### Choose the MariaDB

ProxySQL's card opens on the canvas, under add-ons. **MariaDB backend** shows which MariaDB it will connect to. Our environment had two, so the card said "This environment has several MariaDB databases. Choose the one ProxySQL connects to." Pick one in **Choose a MariaDB**.

<Screenshot id="proxysql/3c-chosen" alt="The ProxySQL card: MariaDB backend, mariadb (mariadb-92xp) chosen, and Deploy" />

ProxySQL gets the MariaDB's app credentials itself; you don't type a password.

</Step>

<Step>

### Deploy

Click **Deploy**. It's created in the environment right away and appears on the canvas.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Backends and query rules

Click the ProxySQL card and open **Settings**.

<Screenshot id="proxysql/5-settings" alt="ProxySQL Settings: Backends mariadb-92xp port 3306 in hostgroups 1 and 2, both ONLINE, and the Read/Write Split rules" />

- **Backends** lists the MySQL servers behind the proxy (`mysql_servers`): hostgroup **1** for writes and **2** for reads, each with its **Host**, **Port**, **Weight** and **Status**. Ours listed the chosen MariaDB, `mariadb-92xp` on port `3306`, in both hostgroups, both **ONLINE**.
- **Read/Write Split** lists the query rules (`mysql_query_rules`). The defaults send `^SELECT.*FOR UPDATE` to hostgroup 1 and other `^SELECT` queries to hostgroup 2. The card warns: "Prefer targeted digests over a blanket ^SELECT rule — generic SELECT routing can break transactions."

**Add row**, **Add rule** and **Apply** change these tables. We left the defaults as they were.

## Connect an app

In ProxySQL's **Settings**, under **Connections**, switch on the app. The change is staged: click **Commit 1 change** (the dialog lists **nginx ⇄ proxysql off → on**) and **Deploy 1 change**.

<Screenshot id="proxysql/6-commit" alt="1 change to apply: Connections, nginx ⇄ proxysql off → on" />

The app gets the connection as variables. Our app already had a MariaDB connected, so ProxySQL's came with a `PROXYSQL_` prefix:

| Variable | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `PROXYSQL_MYSQL_HOST` | ProxySQL's address in the environment |
| `PROXYSQL_MYSQL_PORT` | `6033` |
| `PROXYSQL_MYSQL_USER`, `PROXYSQL_MYSQL_PASSWORD` | the MariaDB app user |
| `PROXYSQL_MYSQL_DATABASE` | the database, here `app` |
| `PROXYSQL_DATABASE_URL` | `mysql://app:…@<host>:6033/app` |

We ran a query from the app through those values. ProxySQL answered on port `6033` and the result came from the MariaDB, `mariadb-92xp-0`.

## Deployments, logs and metrics

ProxySQL's other tabs are **Deployment** (its deploys and restarts), **Logs** (with a pod picker, search and **Live tail**) and **Metrics** (memory and CPU, over **2h**, **24h** or **7d**).

## Delete

**Delete add-on** at the bottom of **Settings** deletes ProxySQL and its data.
