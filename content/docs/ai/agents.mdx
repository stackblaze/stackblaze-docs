An AI agent is a service in your environment that answers with your instructions and can use the tools you allow. Each agent comes with a team chat page, an API, a PostgreSQL database for its chats, and a panel where you change it.

This page creates agents and explains every field of the form. The other agent pages cover the rest:

- [Run and change your agent](/ai/agent-settings): the agent's panel, the chat page, changing its settings, pausing and deleting it.
- [AI Gateway](/ai/agent-gateway): the service that holds a provider key for your agents.
- [Reach your agent](/ai/agent-channels): its API, webhooks, schedules and the website widget.
- [Knowledge, skills and integrations](/ai/agent-knowledge-and-skills): documents, step-by-step skills and outside tools.
- [Test your agent](/ai/agent-evals): evals and optimizations.

We made two agents for these pages, both on Qwen3 4B, a small open model running as an [AI model](/ai/models) in the same environment. One used **Blank** and talked to the model directly. The other used **Webhook triage** through an AI Gateway.

## Create an agent

<Steps>

<Step>

### Open the form

In your project, click **+ Service** and choose **AI agent** (**Chatbot or support agent with your instructions**). **New AI agent** opens.

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/cr-palette" alt="The + Service list: AI model (GPU), AI agent and Backups" />

The form has **Start from**, **Instructions**, **Model** and **Tools**. There's no name field: the first agent is named `ai-agent`, the next `ai-agent-2`, and so on. You can [rename it](/ai/agent-settings#rename-it) on the canvas later.

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/cr-open" alt="New AI agent: Start from, Instructions, Model set to Anthropic with an Anthropic API key field, and Tools" />

</Step>

<Step>

### Start from a template

**Start from** fills in the instructions and the tools. A line under the buttons says what the template is for. Switching templates replaces the instructions, so pick one before you edit them.

| Template | What it's for | Tools | Also sets up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blank** | Your own instructions from scratch | read, grep, glob | Nothing: the instructions are empty |
| **Customer support** | Answers customers from your help articles | read, grep, glob | The website widget, so it asks for **Your site** |
| **FAQ bot** | Answers from your documents, nothing else | read, grep, glob | Nothing |
| **Internal helpdesk** | IT and HR questions for your team | read, grep, glob, web_search | Nothing |
| **Webhook triage** | Summarises incoming events and flags urgent ones | read, grep, glob | A webhook, shown right after **Deploy** |
| **Daily report** | Writes a short report every weekday morning | read, grep, glob | A schedule on weekdays at 09:00 UTC |

The instructions each one fills in:

- **Customer support**: answers about products, orders, shipping and returns, using only the files in its knowledge folder. Two or three friendly sentences. When the files don't cover a question, it says it isn't sure and offers to pass it to a person. It never invents policies, prices or dates, and never asks for passwords or card numbers.
- **FAQ bot**: answers only from its documents and searches them first. It quotes the relevant part and names the file. When the documents don't have the answer, it says so plainly.
- **Internal helpdesk**: IT, accounts, tools, leave and policies. It uses its files first and may search the web for general how-to steps. For anything that needs an admin, it says who to contact.
- **Webhook triage**: for each event it receives, one sentence on what happened, then **Urgent**, **Normal** or **Ignore** with a short reason, in under 60 words.
- **Daily report**: a short status report from the files in its knowledge folder, with **Highlights**, **Problems** and **Next steps**.

**Customer support** adds **Your site (for the website widget)**: visitors there get a chat bubble. It makes the agent public, so it can't use **bash**, **edit** or **write**. **Deploy** checks the site:

- Empty: **Add the site that embeds the widget.**
- `acme.com`: **Enter a site address, like https://acme.com.**
- `https://acme.com/shop`: **Only the site address: no path, like https://acme.com.**

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/cr-tpl-support" alt="Customer support: Your site (for the website widget), and its instructions" />

See [Put it on your website](/ai/agent-channels#put-it-on-your-website) for the widget.

</Step>

<Step>

### Write the instructions

**Instructions** is what the agent is told before every chat: who it is, what it may answer and how. The counter shows how many characters you've used, up to 20,000. Over that, **Deploy** shows **Up to 20,000 characters.**

You can change the instructions after deploying. We did, and the agent followed them in the next chat: see [Change its instructions](/ai/agent-settings#change-its-instructions).

</Step>

<Step>

### Pick the model

**Model** lists, in one menu:

- **Anthropic** (**Claude**), **OpenAI**, **DeepSeek**, **Qwen**, **Kimi** and **GLM**: a provider's API, with your key.
- Each [AI model](/ai/models) in this environment, marked **local model (GPU), no key**.
- Each model of an [AI Gateway](/ai/agent-gateway) already in this environment, marked **AI Gateway …, already here**.
- **Another endpoint** (**any OpenAI-compatible URL**).

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/cr-models" alt="The Model menu: Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM, docs-qwen (local model, no key), Qwen/Qwen3-4B (AI Gateway ai-gateway, already here) and Another endpoint" />

The form starts on **Anthropic**, even when the environment has an AI model. Pick yours from the list.

What each choice asks for:

| Choice | Fields | What Deploy creates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An AI model of this environment | Nothing. The hint says **Connected like any app: no key needed.** | The agent only |
| **Anthropic** | **Anthropic API key** | A new AI Gateway, then the agent |
| **OpenAI** | **OpenAI API key** and **Model ids** | A new AI Gateway, then the agent |
| **DeepSeek**, **Qwen**, **Kimi**, **GLM** | That provider's **API key** and **Model ids** | A new AI Gateway, then the agent |
| **Another endpoint** | **Base URL**, **API key (optional)** and **Model ids** | A new AI Gateway, then the agent |
| An AI Gateway already here | Nothing | The agent only, on that gateway |

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/cr-model-docs-qwen" alt="Model set to docs-qwen: Connected like any app: no key needed." />

**Model ids** takes several ids, separated by commas. The gateway serves all of them, and the agent uses the first. Under each key field: **Saved in the new AI Gateway's variables, not on the agent.**

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/cr-model-openai" alt="OpenAI: OpenAI API key and Model ids, with Paste your OpenAI API key. and Name at least one OpenAI model id." />

**Deploy** checks the fields before anything is deployed:

- No key: **Paste your Anthropic API key.** (or OpenAI, DeepSeek, and so on).
- A key that doesn't start right: **An Anthropic key starts with sk-ant-.** or **An OpenAI key starts with sk-.**
- A key with a space in it: **The key is one line, without spaces.**
- No model id: **Name at least one OpenAI model id.**, or **Name at least one model id.** for another endpoint.
- A base URL that isn't a full address, like `gpu.example`: **An http(s) URL, like https://gpu.example/v1.**

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/cr-model-another" alt="Another endpoint after Deploy: An http(s) URL, like https://gpu.example/v1. and Name at least one model id." />

**Another endpoint** also works for an AI model of this environment. Paste the model's **Base URL**, **API key** and model id from its [Connect](/ai/models#call-it) section. The agent then talks to the model through a gateway, which [optimizations](/ai/agent-evals#optimizations) need.

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/dp-filled" alt="Webhook triage on Another endpoint: the AI model's Base URL, its key hidden, and the model id Qwen/Qwen3-4B" />

</Step>

<Step>

### Choose the tools

**Tools** sets what the agent can do. Each one can be turned on or off:

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **read** | Read files in its knowledge folder |
| **grep** | Search file contents |
| **glob** | Find files by name |
| **web_search** | Search the web |
| **todo** | Keep a task list |
| **bash** | Run shell commands |
| **edit** | Edit files |
| **write** | Create files |

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/cr-tools" alt="The Tools menu: read, grep, glob, web_search, todo, bash, edit and write, each with what it does" />

With every tool off, the field says **No tools: answers from instructions only.** The agent then can't read its documents. When we tried this, it answered from its own knowledge: see [Turn its tools off](/ai/agent-settings#turn-its-tools-off).

On a public agent (one with the website widget), **bash**, **edit** and **write** are greyed out with **Off while the website widget is on**.

</Step>

<Step>

### Deploy it

Click **Deploy**. What happens next depends on the model:

- **An AI model or an AI Gateway already here**: the panel closes at once, with a toast saying the agent is deploying.
- **A provider or another endpoint**: the panel stays open. It first shows **Deploying the AI Gateway with your key…**, then **Waiting for the AI Gateway to start and add its models (about two minutes)…**. Then it creates the agent. Ours took about a minute.

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/dp-gateway-step" alt="Waiting for the AI Gateway to start and add its models (about two minutes)…, with Deploy spinning" />

<Callout>
Keep the panel open until the agent appears. If you close it while the gateway starts, the gateway keeps starting. Open **AI agent** again and pick the gateway's model in **Model**. While it starts, it shows **AI Gateway starting… (checked every 10 s)**.
</Callout>

With **Webhook triage**, the panel then shows the new webhook: **ai-agent-2 is deploying. Its webhook:**, its **URL** and **Secret**, each with **Copy**. Copy the secret now, because it isn't shown again. **Done** closes the panel.

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/dp-webhook" alt="ai-agent-2 is deploying. Its webhook: the URL and the secret (hidden here), each with Copy" />

We sent that webhook a failed-payment event. The agent answered **Urgent: Payment failed for customer C-311 ($49.00 USD) due to card decline after 3 retries. Need immediate customer support or refund process.** See [Start it from a webhook](/ai/agent-channels#start-it-from-a-webhook).

</Step>

</Steps>

## What it adds to your environment

The agent took about a minute to show **Running**. Before that, its card said **Stopped**.

In the **Designer** view, each agent is a service with **open chat** in place of an address. It comes with a PostgreSQL database for its chats (`<agent>-db`). An agent on a gateway is drawn with a line to it, and the gateway has a line to its model.

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/gw-canvas" alt="Designer: ai-agent with its PostgreSQL; ai-agent-2 grouped with ai-gateway and the gateway's shared PostgreSQL; both agents linked to ai-gateway, which calls docs-qwen" />

An agent deployed with its own gateway is grouped with it, under the agent's name. It has no database of its own: its chats go in the gateway's PostgreSQL, next to the gateway's own data. The card shows that database as **shared**.

In the **Apps** view, the agents of an environment show as one **AI agents** card, with **open chat** and how many services it holds. Open the list to see the agent's database and its volume.

<Screenshot id="ai-agent/p-apps-card" alt="The Apps view: the AI agents card with open chat, Running, and ai-agent-5m73-db and ai-agent-5m73-agent-data" />

## Limits

- AI agents are on in production. The platform can turn agents on per plan. Where they're off, creating one is refused, but existing agents keep running and can still be changed and deleted.
- Instructions: up to 20,000 characters. Skills: up to 40,000 characters each. Schedules: up to 20, each message up to 4,000 characters. Widget sites: up to 20.
- An agent is a service: its instances, sleep mode and volumes follow your plan, like any service. On our Business plan test account, **Settings** → **Scale** offered up to 10 instances.
- Every chat uses tokens on the agent's model. With a provider, you pay the provider. With an AI model of your own, you pay for its GPU.
