title: Volumes
description: Give a service a disk that keeps its files across restarts and deploys, choose its size and where it's mounted, grow it, and remove it.
---

A service's own filesystem starts fresh on every restart and deploy. A **volume** is a disk that keeps its files. Open a service, click **Settings**, then **Storage**:

<Screenshot id="settings/storage" alt="Volumes: Shared (RWX) filesystem — every instance mounts the same volume, and Add volume" />

A volume is a shared filesystem: if the service runs more than one instance, every instance mounts the same volume and sees the same files.

Only write to the same files from several instances if your app is built for it. A file database such as SQLite on a volume should run with one instance.

Your plan can limit how many volumes your organization has and how big one can be: the Free Trial has 1 volume of 1 GB, the Free plan 1 volume of 5 GB, and the paid plans have no limit (see [Plans](/billing/plans#the-free-plan)). A volume over the limit is refused when you deploy, with "Your Free plan includes 1 volume. Upgrade to add more." or "A volume can be up to 5 GB on the Free plan; this one asks for 10 GB. Upgrade for larger volumes." Volumes you already have are never removed or shrunk.

## Add a volume

<Steps>

<Step>

### Fill it in

Click **Add volume**. Each volume has:

- **Name**, for example `data`;
- **Size**, **5 GB** by default;
- **Mount Path**, the folder inside the service where it appears, `/data` by default.

<Screenshot id="settings/volume-add" alt="A new volume: Name data, Size 5 GB, Mount Path /data" />

The sizes go from **5 GB** to **1 TB**: 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 250, 500 and 750 GB, and 1 TB. On a plan with a largest volume size, the menu stops there: **1 GB** on the Free Trial, **5 GB** on the Free plan.

<Screenshot id="settings/volume-sizes" alt="The size menu: 5 GB up to 1 TB" />

</Step>

<Step>

### Commit and deploy

Click **Commit 1 change**. The dialog lists the volume with its size and path, here **data 5Gi @ /data**. Add a commit message if you like and click **Deploy 1 change**.

<Screenshot id="settings/volume-commit" alt="1 change to apply: Configuration · nginx, data 5Gi @ /data" />

Once the new version is running, the volume is mounted at its path. We wrote a file to `/data`, restarted the service, and read the same file back from the new instance.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Once it's in use

After it's deployed, the volume is marked **In use**. Its **Name** can't be changed any more. The **Mount Path** can, and the **Size** can only grow: see [Increase the size](#increase-the-size).

<Screenshot id="settings/volume-in-use" alt="The data volume marked In use" />

Volumes are included in the environment's recovery points when backups are on. See [Environment backups](/data/environment-backups).

## Increase the size

A volume in use can be made bigger while the service keeps running. It can't be made smaller.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Pick a larger size

Open **Settings**, then **Storage**. The **Size** menu of a volume in use lists its size, marked **(current)**, and the larger sizes only. Under it:

> Volumes can only grow. Expansion depends on the storage class.

Pick the new size, for example **10 GB** for a 5 GB volume.

</Step>

<Step>

### Commit and deploy

Click **Commit 1 change**, then **Deploy 1 change**. A **Growing volume** message names the volume and both sizes, for example **data 5Gi → 10Gi**.

</Step>

<Step>

### Watch it grow

The service isn't restarted. While the volume grows, the line under the **Size** menu shows how far it has got:

- **Growing to 10Gi (now 5Gi)…** while the disk is being made bigger;
- **Disk grown to 10Gi; the filesystem grows next (no restart needed).** while the service's filesystem catches up.

The line goes away when the volume is at its new size. The page checks every few seconds, so you don't need to reload it.

</Step>

</Steps>

### If it can't grow

- If the volume's storage class can't grow volumes in place, the **Size** menu is greyed out and says why, for example **Storage class "…" cannot grow volumes in place.**
- If the new size is saved but the disk couldn't be grown straight away, a **Volume not grown yet** message gives the reason. The new size is applied the next time the platform updates the service.
- If growing fails, the line under the menu turns red: **Resize to 10Gi failed:** followed by the reason.

A bigger volume is billed at its new size from then on. See [Billing](#billing).

### From the CLI

```sh
stackblaze volume resize data --size 10Gi -p shop -e dev -a web
```

```text
OK Growing volume data 5Gi → 10Gi on shop/dev/web (no restart)
```

`stackblaze volume grow` does the same. The CLI refuses a smaller size (`volumes can only grow, not shrink to 2Gi`). If the volume is already that size, it says so and changes nothing.

## Snapshots from the CLI

`stackblaze snapshots create` takes a snapshot of all of a service's volumes at once, as one group. `stackblaze snapshots list` lists each volume and its snapshots:

```sh
stackblaze snapshots create -p shop -e dev -a web
stackblaze snapshots list -p shop -e dev -a web
```

```text
OK Snapshot group snap-nginx-9agm-1795593648799
1 volume(s) snapshotted
data  5Gi
  nginx-9agm-data-1795593649382  group=snap-nginx-9agm-1795593648799  ready=false
```

A snapshot is ready after a few seconds (`ready=true`). Snapshots are kept on the cluster, separate from recovery points. To get files back as a copy of a volume, restore a [recovery point](/data/environment-backups#restore-a-volume-as-a-copy).

## Billing

A volume is billed on its size, not on what it holds: see **Storage (provisioned)** in [Usage](/billing/usage).

## Remove a volume

Click **Remove** on the volume. The browser asks:

> Remove data from this app? Commit changes to unmount it. The PVC and its data stay in the cluster until deleted separately.

Confirm, then click **Commit 1 change** and **Deploy 1 change**. The service is deployed without the volume.
