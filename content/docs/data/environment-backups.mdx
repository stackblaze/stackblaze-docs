---
title: Environment backups
description: Back up a whole environment — its app volumes and databases — on a schedule or on demand, and restore any of it as a copy.
---

A recovery point holds an environment's **volumes** (the disks attached to your apps) and its **databases**, taken together. You can take one on demand or on a schedule, and restore some or all of it as a copy next to what is running.

This page covers the schedule and volumes. For restoring a database and moving your apps onto the copy, see [Database backups and restore](/data/databases/backups).

## Open Backup and restore

On the project canvas, click **+ Service**, then **Backups** (**Snapshots and restore for this environment**). The row is only listed once the environment has something in it.

<Screenshot id="env-backups/1-menu" alt="The Add to Dev menu with Backups: Snapshots and restore for this environment" />

**Backup and restore** has two tabs: **Backups** (the schedule, **Back up now** and the recovery points) and **Restore**.

## Turn on a schedule

<Steps>

<Step>

### Edit the schedule

Under **Schedule**, a new environment says **Off — back up on demand**. Click **Edit**.

There are three cadences: **Daily**, **Weekly** and **Monthly**. Tick the ones you want and choose how long each keeps its recovery points.

| Cadence | Runs |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Every day at 02:00 |
| **Weekly** | Sundays at 03:00 |
| **Monthly** | On the 1st at 04:00 |

<Screenshot id="env-backups/12-daily-on" alt="The schedule editor: Daily ticked with Keep 6 days, Weekly and Monthly unticked" />

The retention choices are **Keep 3 days**, **6 days**, **7 days**, **14 days**, **1 month**, **3 months**, **6 months** and **1 year**. The defaults are **Keep 6 days** for Daily, **Keep 1 month** for Weekly and **Keep 3 months** for Monthly, capped by your plan: we saw **Keep 1 month** for Monthly on a Business plan.

Your plan caps how long recovery points are kept: 7 days on Developer, 14 on Pro and 30 on Business. The menu lists every choice, but a longer one is saved as your plan's maximum. The Free Trial has no backups. See [Billing](/billing#the-plans).

<Screenshot id="env-backups/11-retention" alt="The retention menu: Keep 3 days to Keep 1 year" />

</Step>

<Step>

### Save and deploy

Click **Save**. Like other canvas changes, the schedule is staged ("Schedule staged. Commit to apply."). Click **Commit 1 change**; the dialog lists **Save backup schedule — when recovery points run and what they cover**. Click **Deploy 1 change**.

<Screenshot id="env-backups/13-schedule-commit" alt="1 change to apply: Save backup schedule" />

The schedule now reads, for example, **Daily · keep 6 days**.

<Screenshot id="env-backups/14-schedule-on" alt="Backup and restore with Schedule: Daily · keep 6 days" />

</Step>

</Steps>

If the environment still has a separate database dump schedule, the editor adds: "Every backup already saves this environment's databases, so saving also turns off the separate database dump schedule."

Nothing is backed up until you turn on a schedule or click **Back up now**: StackBlaze never turns backups on for you.

### When you reach the disk cap

While your workspace is at its plan's disk cap, scheduled backups pause. The sheet says "Scheduled … backups are paused — disk cap reached. They resume automatically once usage drops below the cap." You don't need to turn them back on.

### Turn it off

To turn the schedule off, click **Edit**, untick every cadence and click **Save**. The commit dialog lists **Save backup schedule — turns volume backups off**; deploy it and the schedule reads **Off — back up on demand** again. Existing recovery points stay.

## Back up now

Click **Back up now**. It is staged too: click **Commit 1 change**, check **Create a recovery point — backs up volumes and databases**, and click **Deploy 1 change**. The new point shows **Backing up…** ("usually a minute or two") until it is done.

A point taken with **Back up now** is kept for 7 days, or less if your plan keeps backups for less. To back up only some services or databases, select their cards and use **Backup** in the selection bar (see [Other ways to back up](/data/databases/backups#other-ways-to-back-up)).

Each recovery point lists what it holds and how it was taken, for example **1 database · 1 volume · on demand**. Click a point to open it: it lists its **Databases** and **Volumes** (with the app each volume belongs to), and **Delete recovery point**.

<Screenshot id="env-backups/16-point-open" alt="A recovery point opened: the postgresql database, the data volume of nginx, and Delete recovery point" />

## Restore a volume as a copy

Restoring never touches what is running: it creates the copy next to it, under a new name. Copies are billed like any other volume or database.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Choose what to restore

Open the **Restore** tab. Pick the recovery point under **From**. Under **What**, keep **Everything** or pick **Choose…** and tick only what you need — here, the `data` volume of nginx. The summary names the copy, for example "Adds data-restored from Nov 24, 2026, 12:19 PM, next to what is running. Nothing running changes."

<Screenshot id="env-backups/18-restore-volume" alt="Restore with Choose…: only the data volume ticked, and the copy it adds" />

</Step>

<Step>

### Stage and deploy

Click **Stage restore**, then **Commit 1 change**. Type **copy** to confirm and click **Deploy 1 change**.

<Screenshot id="env-backups/19-restore-commit" alt="1 change to apply: Copy from the recovery point, with copy typed to confirm" />

The copy, `data-restored`, appears on the canvas as its own card, next to the app and its original volume.

</Step>

</Steps>

Restoring a database works the same way; [Database backups and restore](/data/databases/backups) shows it end to end, including **Switch instance** to move your apps onto the copy and how to delete a recovery point.

Restoring from the dashboard or the CLI always creates a copy; it never writes over a running volume or database.

## From the CLI

[`stackblaze backups`](/cli/reference/backups) does the same for an environment:

```sh
stackblaze backups create --wait -p shop -e dev
stackblaze backups list -p shop -e dev
stackblaze backups restore <id> --volume data --wait -p shop -e dev
stackblaze backups schedule -p shop -e dev
```

```text
OK github-demo-gv35/dev scheduled backups: off
Set the schedule in the dashboard (environment → Backups). The CLI never creates a schedule.
```
